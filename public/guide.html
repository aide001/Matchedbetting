What is matched betting?

Bookmakers give new and existing customers free bets to win their business. Matched betting uses a betting exchange to bet against the outcome you backed at the bookmaker. When the stakes are sized correctly, a win on one side cancels out a loss on the other.

The oddsmatcher finds bets where the bookmaker and exchange odds are close. The qualifying bet usually costs a few pence. The free bet then returns most of its face value as profit, whatever the result of the event.

What you need

  • A betting exchange account, such as Betfair, Smarkets, Matchbook or Betdaq.
  • Bookmaker accounts that have sign-up or reload offers.
  • Some starting funds. £50–£100 is enough because your exchange balance has to cover the lay liability.
  • A way to log your bets. The profit tracker does this.

Worked example: "Bet £10, get a £10 free bet"

Step 1: the qualifying bet

You find a match where the bookmaker offers Arsenal to win at 3.0 and the exchange lets you lay Arsenal at 3.1. Exchange commission is 2%.

  • Back £10 on Arsenal at 3.0 at the bookmaker.
  • Lay £9.74 on Arsenal at 3.1 at the exchange. Your liability is £20.45.

Arsenal win: +£20.00 at the bookmaker and −£20.45 at the exchange, so −£0.45.
Arsenal don't win: −£10.00 at the bookmaker and +£9.55 at the exchange after commission, so −£0.45.

You lose 45p whatever happens and unlock the free bet.

Step 2: the free bet (stake not returned)

For free bets, higher odds extract more value. Say you back £10 free at 6.0 and lay at 6.2:

  • Lay £8.09, with a liability of £42.07.
  • Back wins: +£50.00 − £42.07 = +£7.93.
  • Lay wins: £8.09 less 2% commission = +£7.93.

Your overall profit for the offer is about £7.48, with no reliance on luck. Try these numbers in the calculator.

Free bet types

Stake not returned (SNR) is the most common type. A winning free bet pays the winnings only, so you'll usually keep 70–80% of its face value.

Stake returned (SR) pays the winnings and the stake. It's rare, but you can keep close to 100%.

Common mistakes

  • Backing at the exchange instead of laying. Check that the bet slip is pink (lay), not blue (back).
  • Odds moving before you lay. Place the back bet first, then lay straight away. Recalculate if the odds changed.
  • Not reading the terms. Minimum odds, eligible markets and expiry dates all apply.
  • Partially matched lay bets. Make sure the full lay stake gets matched. Check the "unmatched" section.
  • Not enough exchange funds. Before you place the back bet, check you can cover the liability.

Account restrictions ("gubbing")

Bookmakers may restrict accounts that only take offers. To keep accounts healthy, people often place the occasional normal-looking bet, round their stakes (£10 rather than £9.87), and avoid obscure markets.

Tax

In the UK, gambling winnings, including matched betting profits, aren't usually subject to income tax or capital gains tax. Rules vary by country and circumstance, so check your local rules or ask a professional.

Glossary

Back bet
A bet that something will happen, placed at a bookmaker or exchange.
Lay bet
A bet that something won't happen, placed at an exchange. You act as the bookmaker.
Liability
What you lose if a lay bet loses: lay stake × (lay odds − 1). Your exchange balance must cover it.
Commission
The exchange's cut of your net winnings on a market, usually 0–5%.
Qualifying bet
A bet with your own money that unlocks an offer.
Qualifying loss
The small amount you lose matching a qualifying bet.
Rating
How close a back/lay match is to break-even. 100% means no loss on a qualifying bet.
Underlay / overlay
Laying less or more than the balanced stake, so more of the profit lands on one outcome.
Oddsmatcher
A tool that searches for close back and lay odds across bookmakers and exchanges. Try ours.
Gubbed
When a bookmaker limits your account or stops you receiving promotions.
